## Bottom line

The best US story is that US pipeline construction is the physical footprint of the LNG export boom. With the researcher's flags, 8 of the 31 US pipeline rows in construction run to a named LNG export terminal, and they hold 53% of the capacity under construction (211 of 395 bcm/y). Four more lines (Blackfin, Blackcomb, Pelican, Eiger Express, 112 bcm/y) deliver gas to Gulf Coast hubs that LNG plants draw from but also serve other buyers. Counting them takes the flagged share to 82% (323 of 395). My earlier name-based guess was 60%. Lead with 53% and treat 82% as the upper bound (see the new section). Only 11% of construction capacity carries the data center flag, and none of the 9 LNG import pipelines is in construction or proposed. That answers the oversupply-versus-pipelines question directly, and it pairs with the LNG post on export oversupply.

**How this changes my earlier numbers.**
- **LNG corridor in construction: 60% becomes 53% to 82%.** The researcher tied Blackfin (Matterhorn), Blackcomb, Pelican and Eiger Express to LNG export, using EIA's October 2026 project list and company statements. I had called three of those Permian takeaway. The notes say Blackcomb is an "LNG feedgas transport line," and Eiger holds reserved capacity for Corpus Christi.
- **Those four are shared lines, and I checked what the sources support (2026-11-18).** Together they hold 112 bcm/y. Blackcomb ends at the Agua Dulce hub, Eiger Express at the Katy area (with reserved capacity for Corpus Christi), Pelican at the Gillis hub in Louisiana, and Blackfin links to CP2 only through a lateral into CP Express, a separate 40.9 bcm/y row. Each is cited to EIA's October 2026 project list or a company or trade press statement, but none shows that the whole line goes to LNG. Independent search confirmed Blackcomb and Eiger capacity and routes and found nothing on Pelican or Blackfin. So the headline is 53%, the 82% is an upper bound, and the CP2 total below double counts.
- **Open question for the pipelines researcher:** is the flag "delivers gas to a hub LNG plants draw from" or "built primarily for LNG"? The answer decides whether these four count.
- **Flagged capacity exceeds feedgas need.** Flagged construction lines carry up to 323 bcm/y, about 31 bcf/d, against about 17 bcf/d that 126 mtpa of new terminals need. The gap is because capacity is nameplate, lines serve other buyers too, and some feed hubs. So "up to" and "tied to," never "dedicated to." Proposed: 455 bcm/y is about 44 bcf/d against about 35 bcf/d needed.
- **Data centers in construction: about 10% stays about 11%,** but it is a different set of projects (Transco SESE, Ridgeline, Shippingport Lateral, T-15, Lakeshore Lateral and Trident). MVP Southgate and Northeast Supply Enhancement are not flagged.
- **Data centers in proposals: 28% of capacity.** Largest: Mississippi Crossing (21.5), Borealis (20.4), Crossroads (15.3), Desert Southwest (15.3), Southern Natural South System 4 (13.3). Most notes say demand is cited but "no single data center is named." Tiger's Richland Parish lateral is the clearest named case (Meta's Hyperion campus).

**Capacity by terminal, US export-flagged rows in construction or proposed (bcm/y).** Construction: CP2 77 (but see double counting), Rio Grande 46, Woodside Louisiana 66 (two Driftwood pipeline rows, 41 plus 25), Golden Pass 20 (Trident), Port Arthur 20, Commonwealth 15, Freeport 3. Proposed: Sabine Pass 65 (3 rows), Saguaro 57 (lateral and main line, which overlap), Woodside Louisiana Phase 2 47 (plus a 10 bcm/y Phase 3 row entered under the terminal's other name, Louisiana LNG), Plaquemines 46, Alaska LNG 43 (the main line plus Point Thomson), Texas LNG 34. 77 bcm/y in construction (Blackcomb, Pelican, Eiger) and 79 proposed (seven rows, including Borealis and DeLa Express) have no named terminal.

**No new import pipelines.** All 9 import rows are older terminals (Gulf LNG, Cove Point, Elba, Northeast Gateway and others): 6 operating, 3 cancelled, none new. US pipeline building for LNG is entirely export.

**Cancelled LNG-linked rows.** 13 export-flagged rows are cancelled (204 bcm/y), including Permian Pass, Delta Express, Port Arthur Louisiana Connector and West Delta (all 2025) and older ones from 2019 to 2024. Same warning as before: the dates are when we recorded the cancellation, so check announcement dates before calling it a trend.

### The Hormuz angle: gas is not oil

The June oil work (Habshan-Fujairah, East-West) does not carry over. The Hormuz exposure in gas is LNG, and a pipeline cannot carry LNG. What our data can say is how little gas pipeline there is that leaves the Gulf.

- **Qatar has one export pipeline, and it stays inside the Gulf.** Dolphin (Ras Laffan to Taweelah, UAE, 364 km, 32.7 bcm/y, 2006) is the only operating cross-border line out of 59 Qatari rows. The Qatar-Turkey pipeline (1,500 km through Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Syria) is cancelled. Qatar's government said in September it rules out a pipeline bypass because it would need new liquefaction abroad. [Euronews](https://euronews.com/business/2026/09/21/qatar-rules-out-hormuz-pipeline-bypass-and-reshapes-its-sovereign-wealth-strategy), and the original statement from the Qatar News Agency on Sept. 21: [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-9/21/minister-of-state-for-energy-affairs-lng-cannot-be-transported-via-pipelines)
- **The UAE link to the Gulf of Oman is thin.** Taweelah-Fujairah (244 km, operating since 2010) is listed at 1.6 bcm/y, about 5% of Dolphin's 32.7. Al Ain-Fujairah (182 km, 2004) has no capacity listed. A proposed Rakgas line (140 km, Taweelah-Fujairah to Ras Al Khaimah) has no status date or capacity. Capacity is the weakest field here, so check it with the UAE researcher before using the 5%.
- **Oman sits outside the strait and has the gas grid to match.** 20 operating Omani lines (3,725 km), including Fahud-Sohar (33.4 bcm/y, Sohar is on the Gulf of Oman) and the Central Rich and Lean line to Sur, which came online in 2025. Oman's energy minister has called for LNG export routes that avoid Hormuz. [Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Oman-Energy-Minister-Says-Middle-East-Needs-to-Diversify-LNG-Export-Routes.html)
- **Iran-Oman is the one new pipeline that would end outside the strait.** 1,000 km, 10 bcm/y, Rudan County to Sohar, tagged construction. Status is thin (no start year, no owner detail), so verify before it goes in a post. The tracker's only source for construction is a 2023 article, and I found no sign of building work since. Treat it as stalled in any post until a researcher checks it.
- **Saudi Arabia's whole construction pipeline is one project that runs toward the Red Sea.** MGS III (Master Gas System phase 3) is 21 segments, 4,080 km, all in construction, starting 2027 and 2028. Several segments run to Yanbu, Rabigh, Jeddah, Shoaiba and Jizan on the west coast, and Saudi Arabia is 4,080 of the world's 72,372 km in construction. It is domestic supply, not an export bypass, and none of its segments has a capacity listed. The existing East-West gas line to Yanbu (1,200 km, 1978) is the older version of the same idea.
- **Repurposing is real but it is oil.** One Gulf Research Center commentary says Saudi Arabia has moved gas through its East-West line to Western Province power plants and then switched it back to crude. That is one source and I could not confirm it, so treat it as a lead. [GRC](https://www.grc.net/documents/69f058690fe13EvenReopeningHormuzMoreOilExportPipelinesUrgentlyNeededGulfcompressed.pdf) Nothing in our data tags a gas pipeline as repurposed, so we cannot count them. The AGBI item further down says the reverse, that crude moved through a gas line. Neither source could be confirmed, so do not post either version until one is checked.
- **Iraq gas to Turkey is only a proposal.** Basra-Ceyhan gas (1,734 km, 5 bcm/y, proposed, no start year). Turkey has pitched Qatar-Turkey and Basra-Turkey links as part of a Hormuz rewiring. Türkiye's energy minister proposed a gas line next to the Basra oil line so Qatari and other Gulf gas could reach Europe through Iraq. [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/business/economy/turkiye-eyes-new-corridor-to-carry-oil-gas-from-iraq-gulf-to-europe), [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/pipe-dream-turkey-plan-redraw-middle-east-energy-iran-war-hormuz). The bne IntelliNews piece on the same plan would not open, so it is not cited.

Suggested post: "Qatar ships about a fifth of the world's LNG, and its only gas pipeline out of the country ends inside the same Gulf." Chart: a simple map of Ras Laffan to Taweelah with Fujairah and Sohar marked, and the 32.7 versus 1.6 bcm/y bars. The honest framing is that pipelines cannot fix an LNG chokepoint. Unlike oil, there is no Habshan-Fujairah for gas.

### Has the war changed gas pipeline plans around Hormuz? (research review, 2026-11-18)

A search of news, think tank and trade press coverage from March to mid-November 2026. Items marked "opened" were read in full on 2026-11-18. The rest came up in the search and were not opened, so check them before quoting.

**Bottom line.** Lots of talk and no new bypass gas pipeline. Not one gas pipeline around the Gulf has moved from idea to a firm plan because of the closure. Qatar, which has most of the gas at stake, ruled pipelines out. The war has delayed gas projects more than it has started any. The one new cross-border gas pipeline construction we found starting in the wider region this year, in Syria, has nothing to do with Hormuz. (Domestic lines such as Saudi Arabia's MGS III and the UAE's ESTIDAMA were already in construction.)

**Qatar said no.** On Sept. 21, Qatar's energy minister Saad al-Kaabi said "This makes no economic sense" and "We have decided not to use pipelines as an alternative based on commercial and technical criteria." His point is that a pipeline cannot carry LNG, so a bypass would also need new liquefaction plants abroad. (opened) [QNA](https://qna.org.qa/en/News-Area/News/2026-9/21/minister-of-state-for-energy-affairs-lng-cannot-be-transported-via-pipelines), [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/oil-and-gas/2026/09/qatarenergy-rules-out-lng-export-pipelines/)

**Talk only, with no project behind it.**

- Türkiye has pitched reviving Qatar-Turkey (late March or early April) and a gas line beside the planned Basra oil line to carry Gulf gas to Europe (August). The tracker has Qatar-Turkey as cancelled and Basra-Ceyhan gas as proposed, which still fits. (opened) [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/business/economy/turkiye-eyes-new-corridor-to-carry-oil-gas-from-iraq-gulf-to-europe)
- Qatar and Saudi Arabia signed a memorandum of understanding on Oct. 11 to "explore" joint oil and gas pipelines. No route, size or date. (opened) [Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/business/qatar-saudi-arabia-ink-joint-pipeline-deal-urge-keeping-supplies-out-of-iran-war-3230149)
- The Gulf Cooperation Council summit in late April called for more pipelines, and its secretary general repeated that on Nov. 1. Nothing specific. [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/gulf-summit-urges-pipelines-regional-missile-defence-system)
- Iraq and Türkiye launched the Development Road trade corridor in July, and officials have since described it as an energy corridor too. No gas pipeline has been defined. [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/infrastructure/2026/07/iraq-and-turkey-kick-off-long-delayed-development-road/)
- Syrian officials have floated a Qatar to Baniyas line. Not in the tracker, and it should not be until there is more than a quote.
- India's petroleum ministry asked GAIL, Engineers India and Indian Oil in June for a detailed feasibility report on a deep-sea Oman to India gas pipeline, about 2,000 km. It builds on an older study by South Asia Gas Enterprise. (opened) [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/indias-hormuz-hedge-2000-km-deep-sea-oman-gas-pipeline-is-back-in-focus-what-we-know-535894-2026-06-09). **The tracker has no row for this line.** See the tracker notes below.
- Think tanks wrote a lot about bypass gas pipelines, including the Center for Strategic and International Studies and the Gulf Research Center. They mostly agree that pipelines are slow, expensive and do not solve the LNG problem. [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/battle-hormuz-will-reshape-global-lng-market), [GRC](https://www.grc.net/public/documents/69dde98c6275bNeedGasExportPipelinesBypassingHormuzProjectsChallengescompressed.pdf), [AGBI analysis](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/oil-and-gas/2026/08/great-gulf-pipeline-race-which-routes-will-actually-be-built/)

**Real moves, but small or not pipelines.**

- ADNOC Gas has spent months studying export routes that avoid Hormuz, with Fujairah as one option. Its CEO said in November there is no decision. (opened) [Inspenet](https://inspenet.com/en/news/adnoc-gas-alternative-routes-hormuz/), [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/oil-and-gas/2026/11/adnoc-gas-studies-new-lng-export-routes-outside-hormuz/). LNG at Fujairah would need a new gas line from Habshan to the east coast. No such project has been announced, so there is nothing to add to the tracker. An earlier Fujairah LNG plan moved to Ruwais, inside the Gulf, in 2023 ([MEED, May 2023](https://guest.meed.com/adnoc-moves-fujairah-lng-terminal-to-ruwais/)). The 2026 Fujairah study is a new and separate one.
- Saudi Arabia reportedly moved crude through a gas line to the Red Sea for a time. Reports do not name the line. It is most likely the East-West gas line. This is a gas pipeline used for oil, not a new gas route. [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/oil-and-gas/2026/10/saudi-arabia-expands-pipelines-to-reduce-reliance-on-hormuz/)
- Syria started building the 186 km Homs to Aleppo section of the Arab Gas Pipeline on Sept. 29. It is driven by Syria's reconnection with Jordan and Türkiye, not by Hormuz. (opened) [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/menaplus/2026/10/02/syria-begins-work-on-the-arab-gas-pipeline-stretch-that-would-connect-jordans-border-to-turkeys/)

**Delays and damage.**

- Kuwait put a US$3.3 billion gas plant tender at Al-Zour on hold because of the war. (opened) [MEED](https://guest.meed.com/kuwait-gas-project-worth-33bn-put-on-hold/)
- Iraq's floating LNG import terminal deal with Excelerate hinges on the end of the US-Iran conflict. The article says the war "has already disrupted the $450-million contract." That is the article's own wording, not a quoted source, and it gives no new date. (opened) [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/iraq-gas-import-plan-hinges-on-end-of-us-iran-conflict/)
- Iranian gas exports to Iraq stopped in March and only partly came back. [The New Arab](https://www.newarab.com/news/iraqs-gas-imports-iran-halted-due-war). Iran's gas exports to Türkiye were also disrupted. [bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/iranians-freeze-gas-supplies-to-turkey-following-israeli-strike-on-south-pars-433500/). The long-term Iran to Türkiye contract ran out in July 2026, but the bne piece does not say so, so that needs its own source.
- Strikes on March 18 knocked out about 28% of Iran's gas production capacity. Part of that has been restored, and the rest could take until 2028. Iran is bracing for winter shortages. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/16/iran-braces-for-natural-gas-shortages-during-winter-energy-imbalance)
- The search also turned up reports of delays to TotalEnergies' gas project in Iraq, possible slips to Qatar's later North Field East LNG trains, and damage to two Ras Laffan LNG trains. These are LNG and processing plants, not pipelines. Check them before use.
- I found no report of a gas pipeline being physically damaged in the war.

**What this means for posts.** The honest line is: the closure produced pipeline talk, not pipeline projects, and the biggest gas exporter said no. That fits the "gas is not oil" thread above. Avoid saying any bypass gas pipeline is "planned" or "under way."

### Data problems found

- **Mousal-Al Qayyarah (Iraq, 22 km) is listed at 130 bcm/y,** higher than any Iranian trunk line. It is almost certainly a units error. Do not use any Iraq total until it is checked. Flag for the research team.
- **Qatar's 586 bcm/y is a sum of overlapping field-to-plant lines,** not national capacity. Do not quote it.
- **Saudi MGS III has no capacity on any of its 21 segments,** and Saudi Arabia's total operating capacity of 16 bcm/y is therefore far too low. Only say length for Saudi.
- Capacity figures for Gulf rows are sparse, so lead with kilometers, status and routes, not bcm/y, in this section.

## Caveats before anything goes out

- **The Nov file is the first US research refresh in several years, and the Sept file is stale.** Never compare the two or describe any change between them (status moves, new rows, totals) as real-world movement. US rows rose from 497 to 730 with 241 new IDs, which is our research, not growth. Do not cite row counts or decade trends as growth either.
- **Uneven freshness skews US-versus-world comparisons.** The US share of capacity (26%), the 60 vs 20.5 bcm per 1,000 km figure, and the cancellation counts all set freshly researched US rows against regions that may be older, with different segmentation and status accuracy. Treat them as "what our data shows" and confirm with the team before publishing, or lead with US-only figures instead.
- **LNG and data center shares now come from the researcher's flags, not my name-based buckets.** The flags are association, not dedicated use, and the four shared Permian and Haynesville lines (Blackfin, Blackcomb, Pelican, Eiger, 112 bcm/y) move the LNG share between 53% (direct lines) and 82% (all flagged). Lead with 53%. Two rows carry both flags: Trident (construction) and Borealis (proposed). The researcher's pass is US-focused, so a blank elsewhere means not assessed. The earlier 235, 118 and 38 bcm/y bucket table is superseded. Read from the backend sheet on 2026-11-18, so rerun if rows change before release.
- **Capacity is nameplate and can overlap.** Several Driftwood and Sabine segments are phases of one build. Of 126 US in-development rows, 26 have no capacity and 5 have no length.
- **Ownership is empty** for most new rows (`unknown [unknown %]`), so no company league tables yet.
- **Capacity data is the public file, dated Nov 5.** If the final Nov 30 build changes statuses, rerun the scripts before posting. All figures come from the public Nov file except the LNG and data center flag section and the tracker check, which come from the backend.
- Most outside figures are trade press. EIA, Reuters via BOE Report, Wood Mackenzie and OGJ are the sturdiest. Aggregator sites (pulse.bot, greentechlead) were used only to find leads and are not cited.
